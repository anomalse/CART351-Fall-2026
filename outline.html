COURSE DESCRIPTION

In this studio course, students develop interactive projects that take advantage of networked data, redefine online communities, and experiment with new communication structures. The perceptual and aesthetic aspects of digital media are addressed in relation to the technical skill sets required for navigating and understanding the possibilities and limits of networked environments.


CART 351 is a studio class. This Github hosted site will contain all the necessary course materials and/or links to materials pertaining to the course.

Though: All important class related announcements will be made through Moodle. All class assignments will be available on Moodle and students are expected to submit all their assignments via Moodle.


There will be instructor led lectures, presentations and tutorials, however the emphasis will be placed on the students’ active participation: reading, discussion, learning new tools and applying those tools in class exercises, assignments and projects. Students will evolve an idea through ideation, research, brainstorming, sketching, prototyping, iterating, testing, storytelling and collaborating.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

In CART 351, the overriding objective will be to develop meaningful and critical experiences by way of analyzing, utilizing, and evolving upon network driven relationships – interactions between individuals, community and local environment. In class readings and research will emphasize critical thinking, technology, ethical concerns and environmental issues connected to information networks.

After completing this course, students should be able to:

  • Critically and reflectively discuss theories and uses of networks, the traversal of, information extraction techniques and the technologies involved in utilizing, creating, and expanding upon said networks.
  • Feel comfortable using and learning about Javascript, Python and databases, along with libraries and APIs to engage with other available web technologies, services and datasets.
  • Implement meaningful network driven projects utilizing various prototyping and ideation techniques.

The course material and outline is based on the work of Sabine Rosenberg, Pippin Barr and Valerie Lamontagne who have taught the course previously.

STRUCTURE

Class periods will be structured and organized over the following activities: a) technical lectures, tutorials and exercises presenting and demonstrating various web technologies: languages, tools, frameworks and strategies for building networked projects, and b) sessions dedicated to provide students assistance and guidance with presentations, assignments and projects.

The core technical infrastructure of the course will be built around tutorials and assignments which will, at a minimum, require the use of JavaScript (libraries, frameworks, API's...), along with Python (mainly through the FLASK web framework) and MONGO DB. Additional workshops may be scheduled throughout the semester based on student needs and the availability of resources. Students are expected to spend around 4 - 8 hours outside of class every week, in order to complete the exercises, journal entries, mini projects and associated documentation, presentations.

GITHUB REPO

Each student will maintain a public GitHub repository specifically for CART 351. Both your class projects and exercises must be in this repository - clearly organized in folders/sub directories/etc...

The state of the repository (organization, completeness, quality of commit comments etc...) will be used in the evaluation process for this course - so please keep it clean and up to date :)

Additionally, you will need to create at least one HTML page: index.html that will be hosted on GitHubPages containing links to all your course work. Use CSS, images, animations etc.. to create a personal style :)

JOURNAL

Over the course of the semester each student individually (you) will maintain a CART 351 journal.
The journal must be linked to your index.html - as a seperate HTML page: journal.html.

  • Each week you will be required to add a new entry to the journal. The purpose is for you to document your CART 351 journey.
  • Each journal entry is due the midnight before the next class - there will be no extensions for journal entries.
  • You may document your inspirations, motivations and ideas. You may want to comment / discuss one of the readings/videos from the reading list. You may want to comment/discuss any class materials. You can write, sketch, make videos etc...
  • Please note: your journal will be available to all other CART 351 students (and the general public). I would highly encourage all of you to explore /read eachothers' entries... so please keep the content relevant to the class AND ensure that you are always respectful and tolerant.

EXERCISES

Over the course of the course of the semester, starting from week three, students will be assigned exercises, to be completed usually in teams-of-two. Each exercise will need to be completed for the BEGINNINIG of the next class session. It is important and super HELPFUL to do the work in the allocated class time, and participation marks may be lost otherwise.

The instructor will be available during the allocated studio sessions to assist students in the required exercises. Each student (not each team) will upload and link the completed exercise to their CART 351 repository, as well as supplying the url to the tasks via the corresponding Moodle Assignment Activity.

These exercises will cover topics discussed & demonstrated in class. Any given task will have the evaluation criteria stated in its description and will be worth 5% -AND- : just completing the minimum requirements does not mean you will get the full 5%.

MINI-PROJECTS I & II

The brief for each of these will be made available as stated in the schedule. Each Mini-Project will be worth 15% of the final grade, and each student/team will have three weeks to complete it.

MINI-PROJECTS I & II DOCUMENTATION & PRESENTATION

There will be in-class demos/presentations for each Mini-Project and each team member will need to be an active part of the presentation. Additionally, each team member will need to contribute to the documentation rquired for each Mini-Project. The brief for the presentation and documentation of these will be made available as stated in the schedule. For each Mini-Project this part: (documentation & presentation) will be worth 5% of the final grade.

MINI-PROJECTS I & II FEEDBACK

Each student will be required to provide written responses/feedback to class mates in other teams/groups for both Mini-Project I and II. Each student (not group) will need to provide individual feedback to at least two other projects from two other teams for both Mini Project I and II. The details for these assignments will be made available as stated in the schedule.
Each individual feedback submission will be worth 4% of the final grade.

PARTICIPATION

Students are expected to actively participate and attend class in CART 351. You will lose 1.5 grades for each time you are late. Students will also lose grades if the instructor feels they do not participate in class.

Participation can be demonstrated by:

  • Attendance
  • Doing assignments
  • Voicing interesting opinions in class and on the Moodle Forum & Discord
  • Helping and Supporting your peers
  • Providing critical feedback to your peers on any presented work

PROCRASTINATION

Late projects are penalized 15% for each week they are late, unless an extension is requested at least 24 hours before deadline. An Extension will only be given for a reasonable reason - such as Illness with a Medical Note.
